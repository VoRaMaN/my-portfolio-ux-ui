Accessibility statement

A playful site everyone can use

This portfolio is full of motion, sound and games, and it should still work for keyboard users, screen reader users and anyone who prefers a calmer page. I aim for WCAG 2.2 level AA on the core pages: the home page, case studies, the store, the CV and this page. Interactive experiments and games are extras, and their limits are listed below.

Settings you can change

Reduce motion

Your system’s reduced-motion setting is respected automatically. You can also switch it on in Settings (the button in the top bar).

Mute sounds

Interface clicks only play after you interact. Mute them with the speaker icon in the top bar, sidebar or footer. Nothing autoplays.

Minimal theme

The Minimal theme removes the animated background, glows and gradients for a flat, high-contrast page.

Khmer / English

Main headings and navigation are available in Khmer. The page language is set so screen readers switch voice.

What’s in place

  • A “Skip to content” link, landmark regions and a clear heading structure.
  • Every button and link works with the keyboard and has a visible focus ring. Esc closes overlays, case studies and the store.
  • Icon-only buttons have text labels; images have alt text, and decorative images are hidden from screen readers.
  • Toasts, achievements and the download manager announce changes with live regions.
  • Forms have labels, clear error messages, and never rely on colour alone.
  • Before/after and blueprint sliders are real range inputs, so arrow keys move them.
  • Beat Lab 808 is fully playable from the keyboard: Space plays, arrow keys move around the grid, letters play pads.
  • The 3D room has a plain list of every destination under the scene.
  • Body text sits on near-black backgrounds, with contrast above 4.5:1 for normal text.
  • Analytics are cookie-free, store no IP address and switch off when your browser sends Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control.

Known limitations

  • Some UI Lab experiments (particles, shaders, physics) are visual by nature. Each has a text description, but the effect itself is not available to screen readers.
  • The Comet Dash mini-game needs quick pointer or arrow-key input. It is optional and nothing else depends on it.
  • Games that run in the browser (web builds, itch.io embeds and Python games) come from their own engines and may not support assistive technology.
  • Trailers may not have captions yet.
  • Khmer covers the main page structure; long descriptions are still in English.
